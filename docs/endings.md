# Ending Design

## Ending Philosophy

Endings should feel like a change in how the player carries the encounter, not like a score result. The voice may fade, but the last image should leave the player with a precise emotional object: a hand, a corridor, a basin, a door, a lamp afterimage.

## Ending Families

### Warm Endings

Triggered by high trust. The player has treated the voice with care or steadiness.

Tone:
- Grateful, intimate, unafraid.
- The voice names what changed without claiming to be cured.

Scene direction:
- `soundtrack_3.mp3`
- Low fog
- Hand imagery used as release, not grasping.

### Reflective Endings

Triggered by hospital clarity. The player has helped the voice admit the medical memory without forcing certainty.

Tone:
- Clear-eyed and gentle.
- Hospital imagery becomes care-shaped rather than purely clinical.

Scene direction:
- `soundtrack_3.mp3`
- Hospital object present.
- Clinical geometry softens into open space.

### Distant Endings

Fallback when trust and clarity remain limited. These should not punish the player.

Tone:
- Respectful distance.
- The encounter still mattered, but less was integrated.

Scene direction:
- `soundtrack_3.mp3`
- Sparse or empty object tags.
- Fog remains present but not hostile.

## Dream Loop

The dream may repeat up to four passes. Return text should acknowledge recurrence without exposition. Each return should feel slightly more known: the lamp remembers the player even if the world remains unstable.

## Credits

Credits are not a lore dump. They are a quiet wake-up: lamp out, afterimage held, life waiting.
