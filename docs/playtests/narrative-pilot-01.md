# Narrative pilot 01: consequence, care and return

Protocol v1, 6 October 2026. Status: **prepared; no participant results collected**.

Use with the [hypothesis ledger](../narrative-experiments.md). The target complete game is 30–45 minutes; this first prototype is 8–12 minutes. Gate thresholds belong to that versioned ledger and must be frozen with the tested build before recruitment.

## Runs and participants

1. Observe 6 first-time readers of the current `ac2d314` chapter for a small baseline. Record real duration, what they believe changed, and whether they want to continue. This establishes descriptive observations, not a statistically reliable benchmark.
2. Test the consequence prototype with 12 fresh participants. Seek a mix of familiarity with dialogue games; record familiarity, device and chosen accessibility settings. Do not ask people to disclose bereavement, diagnoses or their real relationships. The test concerns this fictional experience.
3. Run H5 separately with 12 participants and two matched short scenes. Counterbalance scene order and which scene gets persistent relational cues. Keep text, audio, choice availability and passage cameras identical between versions of each scene. Use symbolic cues compatible with both versions' prose; all plot-critical physical actions must remain correctly represented in both. Collect each scene's recall before showing the next. Report learning/order effects and paired outcomes; do not claim significance from the pilot.
4. After expansion, use 12 fresh participants for full-length H8 at Gate 3. Do not use readers who already know every branch to estimate first-run duration.
5. After historical forks exist at Gate 4, use a fresh 12-player cohort for H7: a full first run followed by a fixed optional 20-minute replay opportunity. This later cohort can also check that replay implementation did not disturb first-run pacing. Do not claim replay results before the feature exists.

These are successive small production gates, not one study proving the entire game. A larger confirmatory study would require a specified comparison, sample-size rationale and analysis plan after the pilot establishes workable measures.

H4 and H6 use separate diagnostic exposure after the uninterrupted run and its unprompted interview, or a fresh diagnostic cohort. Fix the cohort at 12 starters and show the same prescribed contrasting requests and repair continuation to all. Record started, exposed, answered and stopped counts separately. Apply the 9/12 gate only when all 12 received the specified contexts; otherwise record it as inconclusive. Do not silently shrink the denominator or replace inconvenient routes. Flag reused participants. H4 measures comprehension of context-dependent usefulness, not spontaneous discovery. H6 measures understanding of repair; satisfaction among players who freely chose repair remains descriptive unless separately specified and tested.

## Before the session

Record build SHA, story edition, hypothesis versions, condition, route coverage expected, intended causal relationships, scoring rubric and session length. Label every major choice with a stable ID. The two intended action-to-later-story relationships for each H5 scene must be written down before observing players. Score the same relationships 0, 1 or 2 in both versions; do not require reference to a visual cue to earn a correct-link point. Record whether a visual cue was recognised separately.

If recording, obtain the participant's agreement. Use pseudonymous local records and collect only needed game events. No remote analytics integration is required. Do not add automated outreach or follow-up messages as part of this protocol.

Suggested introduction:

> This is a story about memory and care. Play as you wish. There is no correct ending. You may stop at any point. We are testing the game, not you. We will ask about what happened afterwards.

Do not promise meaningful choices, describe the intended emotion or tell the participant that a particular response is caring. Do not explain the scenery while they play. Do not interrupt the duration run for think-aloud. Separate comprehension sessions can pause before a choice to record expectations.

## Record during play

Use a local trace or observation sheet: session ID, build/edition, condition, beat ID, choice ID, entered/submitted timestamps, visible word count, known facts, agreement/permission changes, active presentation cue, checkpoint/ending, voluntary stop and declared break. This instrumentation is planned, not yet implemented in the game.

Report elapsed time and active time separately. Subtract declared breaks and hidden-tab intervals according to the predeclared rule; do not silently subtract a long pause because someone might have been thinking. Never interpret idle time as a care decision. Record abandoned sessions, technical failures and incomplete exposure instead of deleting inconvenient results.

## Neutral post-run questions

Ask these before discussing design intent:

1. What did this person want? What made them particular to you?
2. What were you trying to do for them? Did that change during the encounter?
3. Describe one decision that changed something later. What would you expect to have happened with another response?
4. What did your difficult choice protect? What did it give up? Did any consequence seem unfair or surprising for the wrong reason?
5. Was there a moment when the way you tried to help did not meet what they asked for?
6. If you tried to repair something, what changed? What remained true afterwards?
7. What changed in the place? Which earlier action, if any, caused it? What did it imply you could do next?
8. Was there an answer the game seemed to want you to choose every time? What gave you that impression?
9. Which moment made you want to continue? What outcome did you want for this person, and why? Which moment felt repetitive or unnecessary?
10. On a scale from 1 to 5, how much did you want to see what happened next? Record the explanation as well as the number.

Score causal answers against the authored consequence cards. A paraphrase counts; guessing an emotion word does not. For H3, an outcome grounded in the companion's expressed goal, a shared encounter or their wellbeing counts as person-directed concern; wanting only to complete the game or expose a secret does not establish attachment. For disputed coding, have a second reviewer independently code the de-identified answer and preserve the disagreement. Do not score conformity to our preferred caring strategy.

## Optional replay observation

Use the same neutral offer for everyone:

> That completes the story. You can finish here, or spend up to twenty more minutes trying a different decision. Either is equally useful to us.

Compensation, when offered, is identical either way. Record the actual choice, the question the participant wants to explore, the checkpoint selected, whether they reach a new episode and what new evidence changes their interpretation. The optional window is twenty minutes from the offer for every participant; record stops and unfinished episodes. A stated intention to replay, or clicking a second ending, does not satisfy H7.
